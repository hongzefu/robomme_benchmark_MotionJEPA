- topic_key: pick_swing_cluster
- summary: Both envs place objects with sequential rejection sampling: a draw either lands in free space or is thrown away and redrawn (spawn_random_cube / spawn_random_target, up to 256 trials each). I rebuilt this sampling in plain numpy/torch and checked it against the frozen specs: all 20 PickXtimes/SwingXtimes candidates in specs.jsonl match bit-for-bit, so the statistics below (3000 layouts per setting) describe the real V4 process. PickXtimes has a deliberate bias: corner_push with corner_bias=0.5 (exponent 1/3 on each axis separately) is applied to all 3 colored cubes (J5). Because of it, 91.3% of colored cubes land in the 4 corner cells (a 3x3 grid over the sampling square), versus 50.6% under uniform sampling with the same rules. In 44.5% of layouts at least 2 colored cubes share a corner cell, versus 14.3% uniform (3.1x); this is the corner piling the user sees (selected ep0/ep3 have 2 colored cubes in one corner cell, ep6 has all 3 in one quadrant). There is also a bug that affects both envs and all tiers. get_actor_obb (trimesh bounding_box_oriented), then _trimesh_box_to_obb2d, gives an already-placed cube a degenerate 2D box (one axis vertical, projected to zero) about 66% of the time. The intended 2 cm gap (6 cm between centers) is then not enforced: 15.5% of V4 PickXtimes layouts and 7.3% of SwingXtimes layouts have a pair of cubes closer than 6 cm (as close as 4.1 cm, i.e. touching). 3 of the 10 frozen PickXtimes candidates have such a pair, including selected ep6 (5.5 cm). Region geometry adds more crowding: 6 cubes, the goal disk and the button share a 0.4 m square that covers only 11.0% of the 256x256 front frame. The button takes the near-robot edge, so colored cubes pile toward the far/camera edge (far third 56.8% vs near third 38.9%). Near the robot, perspective shrinks a 6 cm gap to 8.5 px, versus 16.2 px near the camera. SwingXtimes has no intended bias. Its crowding comes from chance, density, the same OBB bug and perspective; ep3's diagonal line of cubes is a chance layout, with no pair under 9.3 cm. Recommendation for V5, xhard only: in PickXtimes keep J5 (corner_bias 0.5 on all 3) but require the 3 colored cubes to sit in different quadrants; in both envs add exact cube OBBs, an 8 cm minimum center distance for all 6 cubes, and max_trials 1024 (PickXtimes). None of this adds a random draw point, and easy/medium/hard stay byte-identical. Predicted effect in PickXtimes: corner sharing 44.5% to 0, pairs under 7 cm 40.5% to 0, 10 cm three-cube clusters 45.3% to 19.9%, target still at the edge (median 1.62 cm vs 1.75 cm), reset failure 0.97%. Enlarging the PickXtimes region to half 0.25 is an optional extra lever, pending a demo check. It cuts 10 cm clusters to 4.3% and failure to 0.03%.

    - question: PickXtimes 为什么会扎堆生成在一起 (why do PickXtimes objects cluster?)
    - answer: Four stacked causes. (1) Intended corner bias (J5): all 3 colored cubes go through corner_push with b=0.5, so 91.3% of them land in the 4 corner cells (a 3x3 grid over the 36x36 cm area where cube centers can go) and at least 2 share a corner cell in 44.5% of layouts. Under uniform sampling with the same rules it is 14.3%, and for iid points 13.8%, so the bias adds about 30 points (3.1x). Cubes pushed to the same corner or edge line up right next to each other (selected ep6: red/blue/green all along the y=+0.18 edge near the robot). (2) Degenerate-OBB bug: in 66% of cases a placed cube's avoidance box collapses to a segment, so the 2 cm clearance is not enforced. 15.5% of layouts have a pair under 6 cm (the rules intend at least 6 cm) and 40.5% a pair under 7 cm; with correct OBBs the numbers are 0% and 10.3%. The bug alone raises 8 cm three-cube clusters from 5.1% to 13.7%. (3) Density and geometry: 6 cubes, an 8 cm disk and the button share a 0.4 m square. Even an ideal 6 cm Poisson process in that box gives 10 cm three-cube clusters 41% of the time. The button sits on the near-robot edge, so cubes lean to the far edge (colored x far third 56.8% vs near third 38.9%). The distractors fill the middle that the corner-pushed cubes leave empty (|y|<0.1: 56.4% vs 47.2% uniform). (4) Perception: the whole region covers only 11% of the front frame, and near the robot a 6 cm gap is only 8.5 px. Corner bias does NOT make the whole set tighter; RMS spread is 15.9 cm vs 14.3 cm uniform. What it does is create same-corner pairs/triples and edge rows.
    - evidence: Replica (scratch replica.py) bit-exact on all 20 frozen candidates (validate_replica.py: REPLICA_EXACT=20/20). cluster_stats.py N=3000 per setting, seeds 20,000,000+i: V4 same_cornercell_ge2=0.4447, cb0 same rules 0.1433, iid6 0.138; all_min<0.06: V4 0.1547, cb0.5 with exact OBB 0.0; cl08_ge3: V4 0.1373, cb0.5 exact 0.051; poisson6 cl10_ge3=0.4127. projection.py: region = 11.0% of frame. obb_degen_rate.py: 66.1% degenerate.

    - question: Does corner_push push toward the 4 corners of the box so that multiple cubes share a corner?
    - answer: Yes. The two axes are pushed independently, so the joint distribution puts 92.7% of its mass (before rejection) in the 4 corner cells; after rejection 91.3% of colored cubes end up there. There are 3 cubes and nominally 4 corners, but the button usually blocks one near-robot corner and the disk may block another. Blocked-corner trials are rejected and redrawn, so the extra mass goes to the remaining corners. As a result ≥2 colored cubes share a corner cell in 44.5% of layouts (10-candidate frozen sample: 5/10) and share a quadrant in 58.9%. All 3 in one quadrant is rarer (1.4%) than under chance (6.5%), because a corner cell can physically hold only about 1–2 cubes. With b=1.0 corner-cell occupancy rises to 98.8% while sharing is 39.9%.

## blind_spots
  * No V5 variant was run through the simulator or demos; every V5 number comes from the replica. The replica is bit-exact for V4 rules (20/20), but V5 parity must be re-checked after implementation.
  * Demo success impact of the distinct-quadrant rule (more near-robot corner placements next to the button) and of P5's far corners (up to about 0.78 m reach) is unknown.
  * The 66.1% OBB degeneracy rate is measured through the replica's float32 sapien.Pose path. Exact agreement with the real sapien get_actor_obb is inferred from 20/20 bit-exact parity, which includes 9 layouts whose decisions depend on degeneracy; I did not inspect OBBs inside a live scene.
  * 'Clustering' is subjective. I used proxies (shared corner cell or quadrant, pairs under 6/7/8 cm, single-linkage clusters at 8/10 cm, RMS spread, per-axis histograms). The user might react to something else, such as the overall footprint in the frame.
  * The degenerate-OBB bug in other envs (BinFill, PickHighlight, VideoRepick, VideoPlaceButton/Order, MoveCube) was not quantified here.
  * Not evaluated: whether distractors in the middle and colored cubes in the corners create a learnable positional shortcut for the benchmark's models.
  * Recovery-mode draws (inject_fail_grasp) are disabled in V4 (I3) and were not modelled. If V5 turns recovery on, the distractor stream shifts accordingly.
