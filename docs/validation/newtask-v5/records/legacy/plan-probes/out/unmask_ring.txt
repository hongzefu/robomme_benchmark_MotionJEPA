- topic_key: unmask_ring
- summary: V4 places 3 distractor bins in the square ring max(|x|,|y|) ∈ [0.2675, 0.45]. At that count the ring has 6.2 bins/m² over 0.486 m² of visible footprint, while the region inside has 50 bins/m² (8 bins in the 0.4×0.4 region, G2). At the inner density the V4 ring would need 24 bins. Two different implementations exist. VideoUnmask/ButtonUnmask use utils/unmask_distractors.py::spawn_ring_distractor_bins: the main scene generator, drawn after all existing draws; an exact 8-corner pinhole visibility check; OBB avoidance with min_gap factor 0.75; 256 trials. VideoUnmaskSwap/ButtonUnmaskSwap use utils/unmask_swap_xhard.py::sample_distractors: a dedicated stream seeded with seed+0x5D157AC7; a linear visibility approximation that is too loose (4 of the 60 V4 frozen Swap distractors are cut off by 3.8–8.8 px); circle spacing of 0.1178 m between centers; exact swap-sweep rejection (H1). My recommendation is a square/rectangular band that hugs each env's static generation range. Band inner edge = range edge + 0.015 (the xhard min_gap). Band width W = 1/√ρ = 0.1414 m, i.e. one mean-spacing 'cell' at the inner density. The count is ρ=50/m² × the usable area (camera-visible, minus fixed objects), measured on a 1 mm grid: VideoUnmask 15, ButtonUnmask 14, VideoUnmaskSwap 15 (15.40, close to the rounding boundary), ButtonUnmaskSwap 16 (inner range includes the buttons). The cube count is drawn from [floor(N/2), ceil(N/2)]. Colours stay the 3 B2 colours, assigned by balanced cycling. Placement feasibility with ≥500 random layouts per env and max_trials 1024 (Swap envs 512): 100% for all four envs, and 998–1000 of 1000 on the 40 frozen V4 inner layouts. Saturation capacity is about 21–22 bins (min 17–18), so the derived counts have about 30% headroom. Local simulator (in-process monkeypatch, 23 resets/demos in total): VideoUnmask 3/3 resets and 2/2 demos OK; ButtonUnmask 3/3 and 2/2; VideoUnmaskSwap with a tight ring 3/3 and 1/1; ButtonUnmaskSwap with a V5 prototype 2/2 and 1/1. Costs found: (1) the reveal (steps 0–31) is 3–10× slower per step because 22–24 bins overlap at (10,10,10); (2) Swap-env resets take +10–50 s because 45–91% of ring candidates need the exact sweep bisection, even though only 0.2–0.9% of the ring actually intersects a swap lane; (3) the Swap-env cube naming distractor_cube_<colour> crashes (duplicate actor name) as soon as colours repeat. Easy/medium/hard are untouched: all changes stay in xhard-only code paths, and new draws come after existing ones (VideoUnmask/ButtonUnmask) or on the dedicated stream (Swap envs). V4 inner-layout draws stay identical for the same seed.

## blind_spots
  * Simulator samples are tiny: VideoUnmask 3 resets/2 demos, ButtonUnmask 3/2, VideoUnmaskSwap 3/1 (V4 circle rule and square ring, not the exact P2 design), ButtonUnmaskSwap 2/1 (prototype with a circle-approximated spacing rule). All local sm_89 under heavy machine load; demo success rates for V5 are not established.
  * Swap-env feasibility used a fast sampled-SAT sweep approximation, validated on only 84 candidates (83 agree, 1 conservative disagreement).
  * ButtonUnmaskSwap button positions are not recorded in V4 specs; they were resampled from the source distribution.
  * The per-episode dynamic ring (P5) and the P6/P7 performance fixes were not simulated.
  * Button blocking was used as an expectation over positions; per-episode usable area varies (the count is fixed).
  * Wall-time numbers (reset, reveal, demos) are confounded by other jobs (load average about 300–600); the timing ratios are more reliable than the absolute values.
  * Physics stability of about 24 overlapping bodies at (10,10,10) was checked only in 9 episodes.
  * Grasp or push interactions between the arm and ring bins near the region edge were not quantified (no failure-class statistics).
  * The density convention (footprint-area vs centre-area) is a modelling choice; the counts differ (VideoUnmask 15 vs 12).
